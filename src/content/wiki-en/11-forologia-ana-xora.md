This guide provides specialized information and highlights potential issues (Red Flags) depending on your country of tax residency.

---

### 🇩🇪 Germany

The largest Greek community often faces issues regarding pensions and real estate.

- **Pensions:** Based on Article 18 of the DTAA, pensions from the German public sector (Krankenkasse/Deutsche Rentenversicherung) are generally taxed in Germany. However, residents of Greece must declare them in their E1 form as exempt income.
- **Tax Progression (Progressionsvorbehalt):** Germany takes into account the rent you collect in Greece to determine the tax rate on your German income.

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Undeclared Accounts):** The German authorities (Finanzamt) automatically receive data on interest from Greek banks. Failing to declare this interest on your German return (Anlage KAP) is considered tax evasion. If you inherit property in Greece, you are obliged to declare it in both countries, but the tax paid in Greece is deducted based on the avoidance of double taxation.

### 🇬🇧 United Kingdom

In the post-Brexit era and with the changes to the "non-dom" regime, the rules have become stricter.

- **Income Tax:** The [Greece-UK DTAA](https://www.e-forologia.gr/cms/viewContents.aspx?id=1010) applies, which protects against double taxation of salaries and dividends.
- **Remittance Basis:** In 2026, the non-dom regime was replaced by a new residence-based system. New residents are not taxed on foreign income for the first 4 years, provided they do not bring it into the UK.
- **Domicile vs Residence:** You may be a Resident of the UK, but if your Domicile (your "roots") remains in Greece, HMRC may tax your worldwide assets in the event of death (Inheritance Tax).

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Capital Gains Tax - CGT):** If you sell a property in Greece, the UK will demand capital gains tax on the difference between the purchase and sale price, even if the capital gain is tax-free in Greece.

### 🇺🇸 United States of America

The most unique case due to **Citizenship-based Taxation**.

- **Citizenship-based Taxation:** The US taxes its citizens (or Green Card holders) on worldwide income, even if they live permanently in Greece, unless they claim a [Foreign Tax Credit](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-tax-credit) or [Foreign Earned Income Exclusion](https://www.irs.gov/individuals/international-taxpayers/foreign-earned-income-exclusion).
- **FATCA:** Greek banks mandatorily report the accounts of "US Persons" to the US.
- **PFICs:** Greek mutual funds or investments in companies are considered Passive Foreign Investment Companies (PFICs) and are taxed at exorbitant rates.

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Inheritances):** The old Greece-US Treaty (1950) **does not** cover inheritance tax. This means that a property in Greece can be taxed by both countries without a full tax deduction.

### 🇦🇺 Australia

- **Pensions:** The [Greece-Australia DTAA](https://www.taxheaven.gr/news/61907/ypografh-ths-symbashs-apofyghs-diplhs-forologias-metaxy-elladas-aystralias) stipulates that pensions are taxed only in the state of residence.
- **Superannuation:** The tax treatment of "Super" in Greece is complicated. While often treated as a foreign pension, a lump sum withdrawal may be considered capital.

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Main Residence Exemption):** If you decide to return to Greece, Australia applies a "deemed disposal". If you sell your home in Australia while you are already a tax resident of Greece, you may lose the capital gains tax exemption that applied.

---

### 🇨🇭 Switzerland

- **Information Exchange:** Switzerland now has full transparency with Greece (CRS/DAC Directives).
- **Wealth Tax:** Swiss cantons impose a wealth tax. You are obliged to declare the value of your real estate in Greece (based on objective value) on your tax return in Switzerland.

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Source of Wealth Audit):** Switzerland sends AADE data on the "year-end balances" of your accounts. If these amounts cannot be justified by your income, Greece may initiate an audit on the source of your wealth ("pothen esches").

---

### 🇮🇹 Italy & 🇪🇸 Spain

There is a "competition" of incentives for new residents and digital nomads.
- **Spain (Beckham Law):** Allows new residents (e.g., digital nomads) to be taxed at a flat rate of 24% only on Spanish income.
- **Italy (Flat Tax):** Italy offers the "Neo-Domicili" regime (€100,000 flat tax) or 7% for pensioners in the South (similar to the [Greek Article 5B](/en/wiki/05-kinitira-epanapatrismou)).

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Modelo 720 - Spain):** If you live in Spain, you must declare all assets in Greece (real estate, banks) over €50,000. Fines for omission are extremely high.

---

### 🇦🇪 United Arab Emirates (Dubai / Abu Dhabi)

- **New DTAA (2025/2026):** Covers the recent **Corporate Tax (9%)** in the Emirates. Greek shareholders must pay attention to the [Controlled Foreign Company (CFC) rules](https://www.taxheaven.gr/law/4172/2013).
- **Residency Certificate:** To avoid being taxed by Greece, you must issue a "Tax Residency Certificate" every year from the UAE Federal Tax Authority.

> 🚩 **Red Flag (Fictitious Transfer):** If your family is in Greece and you work in the Emirates, AADE considers the "center of your vital interests" to be Greece and will tax your worldwide income (due to zero personal income tax in the UAE).
